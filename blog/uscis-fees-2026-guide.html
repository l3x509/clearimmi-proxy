USCIS Fees in 2026, Explained in Plain Language

Immigration filing fees in 2026 are higher than they've ever been, and there are new fees from H.R. 1 that most people don't know about yet. Here's what things actually cost.

Always verify fees against the official USCIS fee schedule (Form G-1055 at uscis.gov/g-1055) before you pay. Fees can change, and paying the wrong amount — even by $1 — causes your application to be rejected. This article reflects the fee schedule as of the May 29, 2026 edition of Form G-1055 and the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (H.R. 1) signed July 4, 2025, verified as of August 8, 2026.

The $50 online discount — and when it doesn't apply

Most forms save you $50 when filed through myUSCIS.gov. The discount applies to: I-130, N-400, I-90, I-765, I-751, I-140, and several others.

It does not apply to:

  • Form I-485 for most filers (paper only — see the online exception above for certain employment-based cases)
  • Form I-131 filed standalone (advance parole — paper only)
  • Premium processing fees
  • The new H.R. 1 statutory fees (see below)

Filing online also typically gets your receipt notice faster — the system is more efficient than mail.

The concurrent-filing trick most people miss

If you have a pending Form I-485 (adjustment of status), you can file Form I-765 (work permit) and Form I-131 (advance parole) at the same time for dramatically lower cost:

  • I-765 drops from $520 to $260
  • I-131 is included at no additional charge — it's bundled into the I-485 fee

This is real money — hundreds of dollars in savings. Many people pay the full standalone rate by mistake. Ask your representative about filing concurrently.

New fees in 2026: what H.R. 1 added

The One Big Beautiful Bill Act, signed July 4, 2025, created several new mandatory fees that took effect January 1, 2026. These are separate from regular USCIS filing fees, must be paid with a separate payment instrument, and cannot be waived with Form I-912.

Fee waivers: who qualifies and how

USCIS offers fee waivers for many forms if you can't afford to pay. To request one, file Form I-912 (Request for Fee Waiver) — which itself has no fee.

You may qualify if:

  • Your household income is at or below 150% of the Federal Poverty Guidelines, OR
  • You receive a means-tested public benefit (Medicaid, SNAP, SSI, TANF, or similar), OR
  • You can demonstrate financial hardship

Forms that generally accept fee waivers: N-400, I-765 (certain categories), I-90, and others.

Forms that generally do NOT accept fee waivers: I-130, I-485, I-140.

The H.R. 1 statutory fees cannot be waived with Form I-912, regardless of income.

If you're filing on paper because you need a fee waiver, include I-912 with your application packet.

The reduced N-400 fee — and who can use it

If your household income is at or below 400% of the Federal Poverty Guidelines (a higher threshold than the full waiver), you may qualify for a reduced N-400 fee of $380 instead of $760. This option:

  • Requires paper filing (no online discount on top)
  • Requires documentation of household income
  • Is separate from and in addition to the full fee waiver option

What changed in 2026 vs. 2024

  • The base fee structure from April 1, 2024 is still in effect — no major increase to the regular schedule in 2026
  • H.R. 1 fees kicked in January 1, 2026 (asylum fee, annual asylum fee, statutory EAD add-ons); the EAD add-ons rose from $550/$275 to $560/$280 under a later inflation adjustment, also in effect as of this writing
  • The May 29, 2026 G-1055 update added a $24 fee for Form I-102 (Replacement I-94)
  • USCIS moved to a cashless payment system — check payments are no longer accepted at most filing locations
  • A separate $250 consular visa integrity fee was added for immigrant visa processing at U.S. embassies and consulates abroad (this is separate from USCIS fees)
  • The 540-day automatic EAD extension for renewal applicants has ended — work permit renewal timelines are now more urgent

Always verify before you pay

Fees change. An article — even this one — can go stale. Before you submit any application:

  1. Go to uscis.gov/g-1055 and download the current Form G-1055 fee schedule
  2. Or use the USCIS fee calculator at uscis.gov/fees
  3. Follow the payment instructions specific to your form — some forms require separate payments for each form in a package; combining them can cause a rejection
